## The problem

Hours of footage from a group trip to Labuan Bajo, no time to edit it, and a question I wanted answered: where is an agent genuinely useful in creative work, and where is it not?

## My role

I set it up like a production. I was the director and producer; Codex was the editor, working inside DaVinci Resolve through its scripting interface.

## Approach

- **The agent proposes, the director approves.** No approval carried over from one version to the next without a fresh look.
- **Watchable before polished.** A rough 20-minute assembly came first, because it made every later decision concrete.
- **Cut to the music.** The agent mapped the energy of each track and placed the cuts on it.
- **Original media untouched.** Every revision was a separate, verified timeline.

## Result

A finished 14-minute film, after three full cuts and four creative passes. Every creative call stayed with me.

## What I learned

Agents can do the hands; the taste has to stay with a person. A technique that works perfectly can still be creatively flat, so try it on a short reel and get a human verdict before applying it everywhere.
